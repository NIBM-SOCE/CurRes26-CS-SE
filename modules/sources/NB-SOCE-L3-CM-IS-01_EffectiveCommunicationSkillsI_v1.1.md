## 2. Module Purpose and Aims

To build the foundational professional communication every School graduate needs from the first year: clear structured writing, confident spoken presentation, the habit of judging what an audience needs, and the ability to work communicatively inside a team.

Delivery is workshop-based and practice-heavy. Students communicate about their own computing work from the start, so the artefacts they produce double as early portfolio evidence rather than as exercises discarded at the end of the module.

The module is compact by design. It was reduced from 3 credits to 2 at specification stage, and it holds four strands in 100 notional hours. It teaches each until it is usable and does not attempt breadth beyond that.

### Specification Boundary (quoted from 01_Shared_Spine.md v2.3, entry IS.1 - QUOTED, never edited)

> **Covers:** foundational written and spoken communication for academic and professional purposes; audience awareness and adaptation; introductory professional presentation; communication within a team.
>
> **Does NOT cover:** advanced interpersonal, reflective and workplace communication (Effective Communication Skills II, and NB5006CEM at Level 5); entrepreneurship and innovation content (CREST).
>
> *Boundary changes are change requests against Master Decisions, not descriptor edits.*

## 4. Skills Programme and Delivery

### Workshop Programme

| Ref | Theme | Hours |
|---|---|---|
| W1 | Foundations of professional communication: audience, purpose and the judgment of what to say | 3 |
| W2 | Written communication: structure, clarity and the conventions of academic and professional writing | 3 |
| W3 | Spoken communication: preparing and structuring a presentation | 3 |
| W4 | Presentation practice: delivery, question handling and adaptation in the room | 3 |
| W5 | Communication within a team: meetings, progress updates and shared written work | 3 |
|  | Total workshop hours | 15 |

Workshops run as five three-hour blocks (V10 session pattern). Facilitated activities (21 hours) are practice studios applying each theme to the student's own computing work; coaching sessions (9 hours) give structured feedback on developing portfolio pieces; independent work (55 hours) completes the portfolio.

| Total | 100 |
