## 2. Module Purpose and Aims

The module builds the software-engineering process capability of the CS(SE) vertical: how software is taken from an identified need through requirements, analysis and introductory design within a managed lifecycle, and how that work is done in a team. It is the sole home of software-engineering process at the Higher National Diploma level, front-loaded to Year 1 so that every Year 2 applied-development module can assume it; its stage 1 defence (three hard feeder claims, absorbed systems-analysis territory, sole process home and bridging role) is the recorded reason it holds 4 credits. The scope limit is honest: this module carries process, requirements and analysis; it does not carry structural design depth, quality and testing method, user-centred design method or project-management depth, each of which is owned elsewhere in the pathway.

### Specification Boundary (from the record set - QUOTED, never edited)

> **Covers:** the software development lifecycle and process models; requirements engineering and elicitation; systems analysis and modelling; introductory design; team and project practice. [Absorbs system analysis and design - not a separate module.]
>
> **Does NOT cover:** architectural styles and structural design (Software Architecture Foundations); software quality, testing and delivery automation (Software Quality and Testing); user-centred design and usability method (User Experience and Interaction Design); project-management and business practice depth (Business and Project Management Practice).

## 4. Curriculum and Delivery

### Indicative Syllabus

| Unit | Content | Lecture hours |
|---|---|---|
| U1 | The software development lifecycle: plan-driven and agile process models; iteration and increments; lifecycle selection for a given problem | 12 |
| U2 | Requirements engineering: stakeholders and elicitation techniques; functional and non-functional requirements; user stories and specification documents; validation of requirements | 12 |
| U3 | Systems analysis and modelling: use-case, activity, class and sequence modelling; data-flow perspectives; from problem domain to model | 15 |
| U4 | Introductory design: from analysis models to design decisions; modularity, cohesion and coupling at first exposure | 9 |
| U5 | Team and project practice: roles and responsibilities; version-control workflow; reviews and iteration ceremonies; working agreements | 9 |
| U6 | Case integration: an end-to-end analysis case worked across the lifecycle; documentation practice and professional context | 9 |
| | **Total lecture hours** | **66** |

## 5. Assessment

Components per the Assessment Component Taxonomy; weightings confirmed at specification sign-off.

| Component code | Task | Attributes | ILOs | Weight |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| EX | Unseen written examination, 2 hours, on lifecycle, requirements and analysis | Individual; in-class; graded | LO1, LO2, LO3 | 40 |
| CW-G-Home | Team requirements-and-analysis portfolio for a supplied scenario (n=4); individually maintained evidence logs provide the individual-accountability mechanism | Group (n=4); take-home; graded; oral vehicle: Presentation | LO2, LO3, LO4 | 35 |
| CW-I-Class | Supervised systems-modelling exercise under open-resource conditions | Individual; in-class; graded; oral vehicle: None | LO3 | 25 |

The examination is carried because the module's breadth (lifecycle, requirements and analysis as the sole process home) and the integrity of individual grading against group work require it. Weights sum to 100. Reassessment is by failed component. Academic-integrity and generative-AI expectations follow School policy; individually maintained evidence logs and the supervised in-class component anchor individual authorship.
